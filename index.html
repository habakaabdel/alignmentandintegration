What we do

We turn objective reality into ones and zeros and solve problems with software.

Software built specifically for you used to be out of reach. That is the only reason organizations settle for tools that were never built for them. Not anymore.

See the demos Start the conversation

  1. step one

    Research and consultation

    It starts with a conversation about how the work actually runs. Who touches what, where it stalls, and which spreadsheet is quietly holding the whole thing together. The initial consultation is free.

  2. step two

    Development and planning

    Then the shape of the work on paper. What gets built, what stays as it is, and the order it happens in. You see the plan before a line of it is written.

  3. step three

    Building

    Software shaped like that, built on top of what you already have. Your existing systems stay where they are. Nothing gets ripped out to make room.

  4. step four

    Keeping it working

    Software is not furniture. Operations change, funders change their reporting, staff turn over. Someone who knows how it was built has to be there when that happens.

Who this is for

Organizations running on tools that were never built for them. Health and social services is the natural first audience, because that is the work we know from the inside, but the pattern is not specific to it. Any operation that has been bent to fit its software qualifies.

Who you are

    Diagnosis and intervention

    A consultation that maps how the work actually runs, a network intervention supplied afterward, and the impact of that intervention calculated. For clients who want the thinking without the full build.

03

How it works

The engine sits under every build. Three ideas cover it.

  1. one

    It captures what happens as it happens

    The work leaves a record while it is being done, not in a report written afterward from memory. Nobody has to remember Tuesday on Friday afternoon.

  2. two

    It works out overnight what is worth keeping

    Between one day and the next, the noise gets separated from the parts that matter. What you read in the morning is short, because most of what happened did not need to be kept.

  3. three

    A person governs what becomes canon

    Nothing is filed as true on its own. Someone with authority over the work confirms it, and that confirmation is the record. That is the whole governance model, and it is deliberately boring.

The BPSS-SES map

One core, mapped two ways at once: four domains (body, mind, social, spirit) across four nested levels (self out to society). The map places every factor. The network connects them and finds where to act.

Tap a segment of the map to see its diagnostic prompts and intervention moves.

Target the changeable factor with the biggest network reach. Not the biggest factor, and never a central one you cannot move.
Grounded in validated lineage: Engel (BPSS), Sulmasy (the spiritual domain as constitutive), Bronfenbrenner and Ungar (nested systems and resilience), Borsboom and the network theory of psychopathology, systems-dynamics polarity (Sterman), risk versus prognostic epidemiology (Rose), narrative therapy, and two-eyed seeing (Etuaptmumk). Original moves: person-led factor elicitation (POND), network analysis to locate misalignment, and alignment rather than symptom reduction as the goal. The network on tab 2 is one worked illustration. Every real case is mapped from the person's own factors.

Goal at the centre

Fix the goal and its outcome measures before intervening. That is the discipline that keeps the model falsifiable. Every factor is then judged by whether it moves these.
